## Why round 2 exists

Round 1 was **rejected** by the human after rendering in a real browser. The lesson from each miss is baked into this board:

- **"Ember"** (enclosed circle + center dot, cradled by an arc) → read as a **camera lens / webcam / chatbot avatar**. *Takeaway: no enclosed-circle-with-a-dot; nothing that frames a "face".*
- **"Gathering"** (concentric arches) → too literal and arch-y; didn't connect. *Takeaway: drop the architectural arch motif.*
- **"H-Spark"** (H monogram with flame negative space) → too clever; the **H didn't actually read** and the "earth" wordmark was confusing. *Takeaway: if it's a monogram, the letter must be unmistakable; never split or abbreviate the name.*

The human's steer for round 2: **"wide open — surprise me."** So this board spans the full style spectrum — typographic, geometric-abstract, single-object, monogram, conceptual, and a wildcard — rather than three takes on one idea. Every concept still serves the one brief feeling: **being taken care of** (a calm, competent concierge), and avoids chatbot/robot tropes and discount-orange-on-white.

## Concept 1 — "Quill" · Typographic wordmark

**Concept.** The mark *is* the type. A custom lowercase **h** whose right terminal doesn't stop flat — it lifts into a single tapered upstroke, like one ember rising off the letter. The wordmark "Hearth" carries the same warm, slightly humanist cut. The whole brand can live as type alone.
**Why it fits Hearth.** Most grown-up, "knows-the-market" register on the board — no illustration, no icon to misread. The one lift is the entire personality: restraint with a single warm tell, exactly the brief's "minimalism with one confident accent."
**As the agent's presence.** The lifted terminal is the live element: idle steady, thinking = a slow opacity/scale breath on the lift only, speaking = the lift warms/brightens. The letterforms never move, so the agent reads as a calm pulse, not a spinner.
**Favicon / scale.** Favicon = the single styled **h**. At 24px it's just a confident letter with a warm tick; the lift is sized to survive. Letterforms are solid fills, so no thin strokes vanish. Strongest "app icon that's obviously a brand initial."

## Concept 2 — "Keystone" · Abstract geometric mark

**Concept.** Two interlocking rounded planes that nest like cupped hands or a sheltering wedge — an outer plane holding a smaller, offset inner one. Stripe/Vercel-grade abstraction: ownable, warm through its soft geometry, with **no flame, no arch, no face**.
**Why it fits Hearth.** Reads as *something being held and looked after* without any literal motif — pure form. It sits cleanly opposite the "infinite impersonal grid" the brief positions us against: one calm, deliberate object instead of ten thousand listings.
**As the agent's presence.** The inner plane is the live thing being tended: thinking = the inner plane drifts/settles under the outer one in a slow loop; speaking = it brightens. Two planes give a quiet parallax that never looks mechanical.
**Favicon / scale.** Two solid shapes, no strokes — bulletproof down to 24px. The opacity offset is the only nuance; if it muddies at the very smallest size, the favicon can flatten both planes to a single tone and still read as the mark.

## Concept 3 — "Rise" · Refined single ember / spark

**Concept.** ONE elegant, **open** calligraphic stroke: wide at the warm base, tapering and curling as it rises, with a small detached spark lifting off the top. No circle, no arch, no enclosure of any kind — the explicit fix for the rejected "Ember".
**Why it fits Hearth.** The purest expression of the "single calm focal point" motif, but as a free, open gesture rather than a contained disc — so it can't be misread as a lens or avatar. Warm and human, hand-drawn in feel.
**As the agent's presence.** Made to animate: idle = still ember; thinking = the detached spark drifts up and the curl breathes; speaking = a soft warmth pulse along the stroke. The clearest "living flame without fire-clichés" of the set.
**Favicon / scale.** One filled shape plus a tiny spark. At 24px the spark may merge into the body — the favicon variant simply drops the detached spark and keeps the curl, still unmistakable. Holds beautifully at 120px.

## Concept 4 — "Mantel" · Legible monogram

**Concept.** A confident capital **H** that reads as H *first* — two full rounded posts and a real crossbar — with one restrained warm detail: a short tapered rise lifting from the centre of the crossbar (a warmth "cut", not negative-space trickery). Directly answers why "H-Spark" failed.
**Why it fits Hearth.** Most ownable + wordmark-native option, valuable for a one-syllable brand. This time the letter is never in doubt; the warmth is a quiet accent on top of a solid H, not a puzzle.
**As the agent's presence.** The crossbar rise is the live element: thinking = a gentle flicker on the rise only; speaking = warmer fill. Posts stay rock-solid, so the H stays legible while the agent "breathes."
**Favicon / scale.** Strongest favicon for an in-product agent avatar — at 24px it's simply an **H** with a warm centre, instantly an app icon. The rise is the first detail to soften small; an even simpler straight-cut rise is an easy fallback.

## Concept 5 — "Beacon" · Concierge / guidance abstraction

**Concept.** A sweeping open path that narrows as it rises and delivers your eye to a single resting dot — a guiding gesture meaning *"here, this one — I found it for you."* Hints at being helped and at a path/journey, with **no speech bubble and no face**.
**Why it fits Hearth.** The only concept that encodes the *concierge action itself* — guidance, a hand leading you to the one chosen thing — rather than the warmth object. Most literally "buying is a conversation, not a hunt," done abstractly and tastefully.
**As the agent's presence.** Tailor-made indicator: thinking = the guiding path draws on toward the dot (stroke-dash trace) and loops; speaking = the dot pulses as "the answer." Reads as the agent actively bringing you something.
**Favicon / scale.** A tapered stroke + a dot. The dot anchors it at 24px; if the path thins too far small, the favicon can be the dot plus a short stub of the path. Cleanest "motion-native" mark of the board.

## Concept 6 — "Tend" · Wildcard (my pick to surprise)

**Concept.** One continuous open stroke that orbits and folds back over itself in a soft, off-centre loop — attention that keeps *coming back to you*. Not a ring, not a face: an asymmetric single line with one warm thickening at the fold. It tends; it never encloses.
**Why it fits Hearth.** Ties the *name's verb* — to **tend** — to a single unbroken gesture of care in motion. Distinct from everything else on the market (no object, no letter, no flame) and genuinely ownable. Calm, organic, confident.
**As the agent's presence.** The best thinking-indicator on the board: the single line can trace endlessly around its own loop (stroke-dash offset) for "thinking," settle to a still glyph at rest, and warm at the fold when "speaking." One stroke = one helper, always circling back.
**Favicon / scale.** A single medium-weight stroke; the loop stays legible at 24px because there's only one path to read. At 120px the fold's slight weight shift gives it craft. If it ever crowds small, widening the stroke 1pt keeps it clean.

## Top 2 — framed as the human's choice

Both picks fuse the *name*, the *warmth*, and the *agent-presence indicator* into one ownable thing, which is what a short one-syllable brand needs — and both are explicitly safe from every round-1 failure (no enclosed dot, no arch, no unreadable letter).

1. **Concept 1 — "Quill" (typographic wordmark).** The most grown-up and the hardest to misread, because there's nothing to misread — it's the name, with one warm tell. Lowest risk, highest "premium concierge" register, and the favicon is unambiguously a brand initial. **Recommended if you want the safe, timeless flagship.**

2. **Concept 6 — "Tend" (wildcard).** The most ownable and the most alive — a single tending stroke that is unlike anything in the e-commerce space and was *built* to be the agent's thinking indicator. **Recommended if you want the surprising, motion-native signature you asked for.**

Honourable mention: **Concept 4 — "Mantel"** is the strongest pure monogram if you specifically want a confident **H** avatar in-product.

Next step once a direction is picked: lock the single accent in US-E2-03 and apply it only to the focal/live element (the lift, the inner plane, the spark, the crossbar rise, the dot, or the fold), keeping everything else `currentColor`.
