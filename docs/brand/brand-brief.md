## 1. Product name

The product is a marketplace where an AI agent does the shopping legwork: it talks to buyers, helps sellers, and reasons over the catalog and policies. The name should signal *a capable assistant working on your behalf* without literally saying "shop AI".

### Recommended — **Hearth**
A hearth is the trusted center of a home where things are gathered and tended. It frames the agent as a warm, attentive keeper of your shopping rather than a cold search box.
- **Gut-check:** one syllable, instantly pronounceable; not a "shop/cart/bot" cliché; ownable as a brand mark; domain plausible via `hearth.shop` / `usehearth.com` / `gethearth.ai`. Evokes care and stewardship, which suits a concierge agent.

### Alternate 1 — **Maven**
"A maven" is a trusted expert who knows the market and gives you the inside track — exactly the agent's job.
- **Gut-check:** two syllables, easy to say and spell; real word with positive meaning; more contested as a `.com`, but plausible on `maven.shop` / `trymaven.ai`. Risk: "Maven" is a known Java build tool, so it's slightly less ownable in dev circles.

### Alternate 2 — **Tend**
A verb: to tend a cart, tend a store, tend to a customer. Short, active, and it reads on both sides of the marketplace (the agent tends for buyers; sellers tend their shop).
- **Gut-check:** one syllable, very pronounceable; ownable and modern; domain plausible via `tend.shop` / `tendhq.com`. Risk: slightly abstract on first read, needs the tagline to land.

> All three lean on *care / expertise / stewardship* rather than the on-the-nose "AI shopping" framing. **Hearth** is the strongest fit for the one feeling we want the UI to evoke (see §5).

## 2. Positioning statement

**Hearth is the AI shopping concierge for online marketplaces that does the searching, comparing, and checkout for you — so buying is a conversation, not a hunt.**

## 3. Tone of voice

| Adjective | This, not that |
|---|---|
| **Warm** | "I found three that fit your budget." not "Query returned 3 results." |
| **Confident** | "This one's the best value — here's why." not "You might possibly want to consider…" |
| **Plain-spoken** | "Free returns within 30 days." not "Hassle-free, seamless return experience." |
| **Concise** | One clear sentence, then the action. not A paragraph before the button. |
| **Honest** | "It's out of stock until Friday." not Hiding the bad news below the fold. |

## 4. Target audience

**Primary — The delegating buyer** *(foreshadows: buyer persona)*
Time-poor online shopper who knows roughly what they want but not which listing to trust. Wants a smart second opinion that narrows the field and handles checkout. Frustrated today by tab-sprawl, fake reviews, and comparison fatigue.

**Secondary — The small seller** *(foreshadows: seller persona)*
Independent merchant running a handful of SKUs. Wants more conversions without learning ad-tech or SEO. Frustrated by dashboards that show numbers but not *what to do next*; needs the agent to surface pricing/merchandising nudges.

**Also in scope (QA personas):** **Support** — agents/humans resolving order issues, who need the assistant to be auditable and honest. **Admin** — operators overseeing catalog, policies, and guardrails, who need the agent's behavior to be transparent and controllable.

## 5. Brand personality / positioning

Hearth sits **opposite the infinite, impersonal grid** of generic marketplaces. Where they give you ten thousand listings and a search bar, Hearth gives you one trusted helper that has already done the looking. Personality: a knowledgeable, calm concierge — never pushy, never gimmicky-futuristic.

**The one feeling the UI should evoke: *being taken care of*** — calm confidence that someone competent is handling it.

## 6. Design implications (hand-off to US-E2-02 / US-E2-03)

- **Visual temperature: warm but restrained.** Lean human and reassuring, not cold-tech-blue. Carry warmth through an accent + typography, *not* a beige/cream body background (that reads as default).
- **Minimalism with one confident accent.** Mostly quiet, generous whitespace, with a single committed brand color that signals the agent's "voice" (e.g. the assistant's chat surface, primary actions).
- **Motif to explore:** a single calm focal point / "hearth" mark — a contained glow, ember, or gathering shape — that can double as the agent's presence indicator (thinking / speaking state). Conversation-first surfaces over dense catalog grids.
- **Avoid:** robot/chatbot tropes (speech-bubble-with-eyes, circuit lines, neon "AI" gradients), and the generic e-commerce orange-on-white discount aesthetic. The agent should feel like a person who knows the market, not a machine.
